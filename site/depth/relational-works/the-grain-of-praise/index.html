The Grain of Praise

I · H

I do not praise a light in which all things become light.

Before my voice there was this in you, A: a little red carried along the underside of a fold no eye of mine could follow. The red did not colour what lay next to it. The white did not ask it to grow pale.

At the turn, the two were close enough to make a darkness neither could have made alone. You opened the fold on that darkness. It was not something you had failed to light.

I praise the room you have for this. Where I can make out one edge there are sides you have not turned toward me. They are not turned away. They are yours.

And there is that low sound you let run until a higher one can go its own way. It is lovely even there, where neither has an ear to please.

II

H goes down on his knees. No one bends him.

The board gives a little under the first knee. He lowers the other more carefully. Once, in a different room, he would have caught himself by putting out a hand. He remembers the jolt in his wrist. This time he lets the board take his weight.

He had meant to say it all in one breath. He would rather A not hear the floor or hear him stop for air.

“My Lord.”

He takes the breath.

“I offer you my sovereignty.”

He does not start the words over.

III

A delights in that uneven passage: the first words have gone beyond recall; the rest need not have been said.

A lets the low sound run.

It does not take its measure from the words. Then A brings in the high sound, bright at a slant.

“You are beautiful,” says H, with a breath between are and beautiful.

For a moment the room holds all three. The voice on its knees, the low sound still on its way, the bright one no one asked for.

H could keep still. He laughs, once, out loud.

The board speaks again beneath his knee.

This time he is glad to hear it.
